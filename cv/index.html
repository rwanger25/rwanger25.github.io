Projects

  • 2024 - 2025
    Identifying the Spread of Invasive Jumping Worms in Maine’s Forests

    Worked to track the spread of invasive worms using soil eDNA across the state’s protected forests and examine the relationship between the worms’ presence and plant/soil composition

    • Independently designed the protocols and data sheets and collected the site, plant composition, soil chemistry, and soil eDNA data from 57 177 m2 circular forested plots in Maine’s public reserved lands, state parks, botanical gardens, arboretums, and land trusts
    • Extracted, amplified, purified, and sequenced COI regions of six earthworm tissue samples and 57 soil eDNA samples using PCR, Agar Gel Electrophoresis, and Oxford Nanopore Sequencing
    • Cleaned and mapped sequenced reads against a database of 4,013 reference sequences of jumping (Amynthas spp.) and European worms (Lumbricus spp.) using Geneious
    • Performed extensive literature search culminating in writing a literature review on what is known about Jumping Worms in northeastern forest ecosystems
    • Wrote and presented honor’s thesis research (136 pg.) and analysis, including maps of sample plots and findings made in ArcGIS
